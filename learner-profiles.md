---
title: Learner Profiles
---

These profiles describe the people this lesson is for. They come from the target audience notes the authors wrote during lesson design. The names are fictional.

## Maya, a researcher with software ready to share

Maya is a postdoctoral researcher. She wrote scripts and a small tool to analyze her lab's data, and other labs have asked to use them. She knows how to write and run code. She has heard that she needs a license, but she does not know the difference between the options, and the number of choices feels confusing.

She wants to share the software and be sure that she picks a license that fits her goals. She also needs to know when to ask her institution before she decides, for example when the university owns the code.

This lesson helps her choose and apply a license with confidence. It is the main audience, because it matches the people who contact a university open source program office (OSPO) with a project already in hand.

## Dev, a curious newcomer

Dev works in a campus research group and uses open source tools every day. He has never thought about the license behind them. A colleague mentioned that "no license" can cause problems, and he wants to understand why.

He does not have a project to release yet. He wants a clear picture of what an open source license is, what the main types allow, and what to watch for when he reuses other people's code.

This lesson gives him a foundation for later decisions about using and sharing open source software.

## Sam, a learner who wants to know why

Sam supports research computing for a department. People ask Sam which license to use. Sam can follow a flowchart, but wants to understand the reasons behind the different choices, so that the advice holds up in new situations.

This lesson gives Sam the reasoning behind permissive and copyleft licenses, including the cases where they conflict.

## What all three share

- They need no knowledge of licensing before the lesson.
- They know, at a basic level, what open source software is.
- They want to make informed decisions, not just follow a rule.
